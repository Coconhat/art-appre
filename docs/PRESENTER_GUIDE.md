# Presenting *Gaano Kalayo?*

A scroll-driven artwork in ten parts. Plan on 8–12 minutes if you scroll through everything,
or about a minute per part if you narrate. The full meaning of every element is on the
**Artist statement** page (`/statement`, linked top right), written so you can present from it.

## Before class

1. `npm install` (first time only), then `npm run build` and `npm run start`.
   The production build is smoother than `npm run dev`. Open http://localhost:3000.
2. Fill in your names, school and term in `src/content/credits.ts`.
3. Re-check the numbers in `src/content/facts.ts` against the links on `/statement#sources`.
   They were checked on 27–28 October 2026. Casualty figures change daily.
4. Use a laptop plugged in, Chrome or Edge, full screen (F11). Turn on sound (top right)
   if the room has speakers; it is synthesised live, there are no audio files.
5. Rehearse once. On opening, the page builds every 3D scene before it lets you scroll (the line
   under the title counts them in); give it a few seconds on a slow connection.

## Controls

| Action | How |
| --- | --- |
| Next / previous screen | Scroll, or a presenter clicker (Page Down / Page Up) |
| Jump to next / previous part | `]` and `[` |
| Jump to any part | The list on the right edge (wide screens) |
| Steer the child in *Lakad* | Move the mouse, or ← →; on a phone, swipe on the left or right side |
| Light candles in *Bilang* | Press and hold the button, or hold Space |
| Fly the kite in *Kalayaan* | Move the mouse, or the arrow keys; type a name and press *Let it fly* |

## Talking points, part by part

- **Simula (Beginning).** We were asked not to open with the slogan, so the work opens with a question:
  how far is far? Nothing tells you where this is yet.
- **Tahanan (Home).** We start with *your* home. Pick three things to carry. (Ask the class what they packed.)
- **Susi (The key).** Most people take the key. In 1948, about 750,000 Palestinians did, and their
  grandchildren still keep them. This is the first time Palestine is named.
- **Boto (The vote).** The Philippines voted yes to partition in 1947, three days after our delegate
  spoke against it. We circle our own name: this story includes us.
- **Pader (The wall).** 109 years of history painted on concrete, including 7 October 2023.
- **Lakad (The walk).** Scroll to walk. You can steer, but you can't leave the strip, and you can't go back
  (try scrolling up). Every path ends at the wall at Rafah.
- **Bilang (The count).** One light per person. The small lights are children. Hold the button: one
  candle per second would take 20 hours and 43 minutes.
- **Sining (The art).** Five works that would not disappear. Point out that the work has never shown
  red, green, black and white together.
- **Sinulid (The thread).** 8,843 km, sewn shut with red thread: our caregivers, our seafarers, our families.
  Two eight-pointed stars: our sun and the tatreez star.
- **Halaga (The price).** *Halaga* means both price and worth. Ripples spread from Gaza to the Red Sea,
  Hormuz, the world, the Philippines, and you. Then the price board: diesel ₱56.50 → ₱153.70 a litre
  (April 2026, the highest ever). Energy emergency, inflation 2.4% → 7.2%, 1.34 million at risk of
  poverty. Close on: "We pay in pesos. Gaza pays in lives."
- **Kalayaan (Freedom).** The colours come back as the flag, in three languages. Fly a kite. He has
  not turned around yet.

## If something goes wrong

- **A 3D scene is blank:** scroll away and back; it re-creates itself. On very old laptops, the
  walk is the heaviest scene; the rest of the piece still works without it.
- **No sound:** browsers only allow sound after a click. Click *Sound off* to turn it on.
- **Projector looks washed out:** the piece is dark. Turn the room lights down for *Susi* and *Bilang*.
